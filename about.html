Health knowledge shouldn't stop at borders, or at jargon.

Making Health Human connects trustworthy health resources and explains research in plain language, with African and diaspora communities especially in mind.

About MHH

I'm Nomi (Nomcebo Jele), a science communicator from Pretoria, based in Germany since 2017. MHH brings together useful resources from scientific societies, patient organisations and public-health institutions — and helps make sense of who they're for, why they're useful and where to go next.

It's grounded in a simple idea inspired by Ubuntu: we understand health better together.

Why I started this

Working in health communication, I kept coming across brilliant courses, research, tools and patient resources — often sitting on completely different websites and reaching very different audiences. I started MHH because I wanted one place where useful health knowledge could be easier to discover, understand and share.

How MHH thinks about communication

Good science cannot improve people's lives if it never reaches them in a form they can understand, question and use. MHH starts by listening to what people are actually asking, then works to make reliable evidence clearer without stripping away its complexity, limitations or uncertainty. It also recognises that trust does not always come from institutions: researchers, clinicians, nurses, pharmacists, patient advocates and people with lived experience can all help knowledge travel further when they are supported to communicate responsibly. When credible voices leave these spaces empty, less reliable ones are often ready to fill them.

Who I am — and who I'm not

I'm a communicator, not a clinician. MHH provides educational information and research communication; it does not diagnose, treat or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.

Transparency

I work in communications at the European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD). Making Health Human is my independent personal project and does not speak for or represent EASD. EASD resources appear where they meet the same criteria used to select resources from every other organisation.

How MHH chooses resources

  • Credible source: preference for scientific societies, public-health bodies, established patient organisations and major research institutions.
  • Useful to someone specific: we try to say who a resource is actually for before you click.
  • Original where possible: MHH links back to the people and organisations that produced the work.
  • Accessible: free and multilingual resources are highlighted when available.
  • Broader than one health system: MHH deliberately looks for African, diaspora and international perspectives.
  • Transparent: relevant affiliations and limitations are disclosed.
